      How the scale works

      Low water and small surf make the firmest, widest driving lane. The score adds a tide step (how far up the beach the water sits, scaled to this station's normal range) to a surf step, and adds one more step when the tide is rising past mid-tide because the lane closes behind you. Night hours are shaded on the chart.

      EasyWater near the low mark, surf under 2 ft. Wide packed lane.
      DoableMid-tide or 2–3.5 ft surf. Lane narrows, keep moving.
      ToughUpper half of the tide or 3.5–5 ft surf. Soft sand, washovers at the cuts.
      AvoidHigh water, big surf, or both. Wait for the drop.
      Tides are astronomical predictions (no storm surge, wind or swell setup). Surf is a model forecast for open water about 2 nmi off the beach; breaking waves at the shore differ. Check the park's beach driving status and the NWS surf zone forecast before you go, and treat this page as a planning aid, not a navigation tool.
